# Guideline 4.3(a) response package — Cab Hustle 1.0.0 (1068)

Apple rejected Cab Hustle 1.0.0 (1068) on 2026-10-08 under Guideline 4.3(a)
(Design - Spam), submission `a20af53a-8b80-4f7a-8f2e-4ccd11d6e244`, iPhone 17
Pro Max, and asked for a reply in App Store Connect.

This file is the human half of the response: a paste-ready Resolution Center
reply and a listing review checklist. The pipeline half is
`taxi_game/fastlane/review_notes.txt`, written onto every submitted version by
the submit lane (issue #211); the App Store Connect steps are tracked in
issue #212.

## Paste into the Resolution Center

> Thank you for reviewing Cab Hustle and for the detail in the 4.3(a)
> feedback. We would like to explain what makes the app its own work, and we
> would welcome a second look.
>
> Cab Hustle is an independently developed arcade game, written from scratch
> in Flutter with the Flame engine. Its code, its top-down sprite art, and
> its audio are original to this project — the audio is CC0-licensed packs
> listed in the app's bundled license inventory plus sounds synthesized in
> this repository. It builds on no third-party app template and shares no
> assets or code with another app.
>
> Where it shares a genre with other driving games, its design is its own
> and visible in the first session:
>
> - Daily Shift: one course per day, the same for every player, with a ghost
>   of your best attempt to race; the day's attempt and result are saved
>   on-device.
> - Endless Shift: a run with three-strike lives that ends only when a third
>   crash forfeits whatever is unbanked; every dropoff is a bank-or-push
>   decision.
> - A near-miss economy: shaving past traffic pays, so precision driving is
>   what scores.
> - Vehicles with real handling differences, a garage progression, records,
>   lifetime stats, and achievements.
>
> The app has no account system, makes no network calls, and shows no ads or
> in-app purchases; all progress lives on the device. The version now also
> carries App Review notes summarizing this, and the listing's screenshots
> and text have been reviewed to surface these modes. We would appreciate a
> reconsideration of the 4.3(a) finding, and we are happy to answer any
> question or provide anything else you need.

## Listing review checklist (App Store Connect → the version)

- [ ] Name / subtitle: keep "Cab Hustle"; lead the subtitle with the modes
      ("Daily & Endless Shifts") rather than generic taxi wording.
- [ ] Description: open with what is unique — Daily Shift, Endless Shift with
      bank-or-push, near-miss scoring, garage with handling differences — not
      with "3D taxi simulator" phrasing that reads like every other listing.
- [ ] Keywords: prefer mode and feature words over the genre words every taxi
      game uses.
- [ ] Screenshots: at least the first two should show Daily Shift and a
      bank-or-push moment, so the reviewer sees the game before reading a
      word.
- [ ] App Review Information → Notes: confirm the review notes are present
      (the submit lane writes `fastlane/review_notes.txt` there).

## Why a human sends this

Issue #93: human rejections are never auto-resubmitted. Issue #102: while the
rejected submission sits in `UNRESOLVED_ISSUES`, the release gate ships
TestFlight only (`REVIEW_STUCK`). Replying and resubmitting are deliberate
acts in App Store Connect — the checklist for them is issue #212.
