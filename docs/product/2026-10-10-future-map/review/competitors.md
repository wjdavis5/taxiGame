## 2. Cross-game synthesis: what the genre's best actually do

1. **Near-miss is the genre's universal reward channel.** Named, threshold-based bonuses are table stakes: close overtakes >100 km/h (Traffic Racer, Traffic Rider), wrong-way bonuses (Traffic Racer Two-Way; Moto Rider GO), wheelies (Traffic Rider), close calls (Highway Rider), drift/stunt/near-miss fare combos (Crazy Taxi). Sources as in §1.
2. **The failure-to-progress conversion is a purchasable moment.** Drift Boss sells Car Insurance ("one extra chance after falling… respawn and continue"); Traffic Rider sells continues with coins; Moto Rider GO makes "extra lives" a bike stat. Failure is a decision, not just a reset. (Poki page; Apple store/review sources as in §1.)
3. **Garage progression is the long tail, and handling differences are the content.** Drift Boss: fire truck feels big/slow, police car quick/light; Moto Rider GO: tune speed/braking/lives; Traffic Racer: 40+ cars, cash economy. (Sources as in §1.)
4. **The modern daily loop is three-part: missions, passive income, login rewards.** Traffic Racer 4.0 added daily missions; Traffic Rider 2.0 added daily quests + passive income + a bank; Drift Boss has Daily Rewards plus a post-crash Spin to Win. (Store pages; Poki.)
5. **Mode variety converts one idea into a week of goals.** Traffic Racer: Endless/Two-Way/Time Trial/Police Chase/Free Ride; Traffic Rider: career/endless/time trial/free ride; Moto Rider GO: 4 modes. (Store pages.)
6. **Leaderboards are the prestige layer.** Game Center in Traffic Racer, Traffic Rider, Highway Rider, Dashy Crashy; weekly online ladders in Moto Rider GO. (Store pages.)
7. **Presentation identity is deliberately engineered, not incidental.** Dashy Crashy's low-poly was a design hack for traffic density plus an announcer for voice; Traffic Rider chose FPV; Moto Rider GO ships a dashboard. Identity is what a screenshot proves. (App Store story; store pages.)
8. **Update cadence is part of the pitch.** Traffic Racer v4.0 (Dec 2025), Traffic Rider v2.10 (Dec 2025), Moto Rider GO (Dec 2025) all refreshed within the last year, most of them adding daily/retention features. (Store pages.)
9. **Scale anchors (Android installs as proxy):** Traffic Racer 470M installs and 4.58/5 from 5.46M ratings; Traffic Rider 500M+; Moto Rider GO 100M+ with Editors' Choice. iOS quality anchors: Traffic Rider 4.6 from 48K ratings; Rush Hour 3D 4.6 from 88K; Dashy Crashy GO 4.6 from 2.1K. (AppBrain; Play; store pages.)
10. **Every studied game is free with ads and/or IAP.** There is no premium, no-ads, no-IAP vertical driving arcade anywhere in this set. (All cited store pages.)

## 4. "The best vertical scroller taxi game in the world" — a concrete bar

Each element below is drawn from a cited game, so the bar is measurable rather than adjectival:

1. **Control:** one thumb, first run inside one tap; a single input can carry a hit (Drift Boss's whole scheme; Dashy Crashy's "swipe, don't crash"). Cab Hustle's steering must stay at ≤2 affordances.
2. **Scoring:** at least three named bonus events with live feedback — near-miss above a speed threshold (Traffic Racer/Traffic Rider), a wrong-way or high-risk bonus (Moto Rider GO), a trick (Traffic Rider wheelies) — plus one taxi-native event where fare/tips scale with stunts (Crazy Taxi combos; Taxi Drift Mania tips).
3. **A risk dial per run:** Crazy Taxi's clock extension per delivery and Drift Boss's keep-going-or-spin are the genre's versions; Cab Hustle's bank-or-push is this, and must be visible in the HUD as a number you can lose.
4. **Meta with teeth:** a garage where handling differences are felt (Drift Boss vehicle feel) and upgrades are explicit stats (Moto Rider GO speed/braking/extra lives; Traffic Racer 40+ cars). Around 10+ vehicles with a spend plan.
5. **A daily hook with a specific payout:** daily missions (Traffic Racer), daily quests + passive income (Traffic Rider), daily rewards + spin (Drift Boss). Cab Hustle's Daily Shift + ghost is already rarer than all of them — its payout (streak, title, records) has to be worth a login.
6. **Breadth:** at least one mode beyond endless — a career ladder with objectives (Traffic Rider 90+), or a one-life time attack (all three) — so "best in the world" players have structure.
7. **Presentation identity a screenshot can prove:** one glance says "the taxi game" (Dashy's announcer tone; Traffic Rider's FPV; Moto Rider GO's dashboard). For a cab game, that means streets read as a taxi's city and the cab is the hero object.
8. **Sustained quality bar:** ≥4.6 stars at scale (Traffic Rider 4.6/48K; Rush Hour 3D 4.6/88K; Dashy 4.6/2.1K) and meaningful updates at least a couple of times a year (observed across 2024–2025 releases).
9. **A one-line differentiator reviewers and store visitors can repeat:** e.g., "the taxi game where you choose when to bank the shift" or "the only no-ads, no-IAP arcade cab game." Nothing in the studied set can claim the second one.

## 6. Apple's 4.3 / 4.3(a): what makes a game get called non-unique, and what clears it

### 6.1 The rule, verbatim (fetched 10 Oct 2026)

- **4.3(a):** "Don't create multiple Bundle IDs of the same app (for example, submitting a separate map app for every city in the world instead of a single worldwide map that allows users to search any city)… If your app has different versions for specific locations, sports teams, universities, etc., consider submitting a single app and providing the variations using in-app purchase."
- **4.3(b):** "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available. Opportunistically creating variants of existing app categories or popular apps degrades App Store discovery… Certain kinds of apps, such as dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, and fortune telling, are well established on the App Store and we will not accept new submissions unless they offer a meaningfully different or improved experience. …Repeated submissions of this kind may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program."
- Adjacent: 4.1(a) Copycats ("Don't simply copy the latest popular app… or make some minor changes…"); 4.2.6 (apps from commercialized templates/app-generation services are rejected unless submitted by the content provider).

Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ (direct)

### 6.2 What the rejection letter actually says

The standard reviewer message, drawn from developer forum threads this session: "We noticed the app shares a similar binary, metadata, and/or concept as apps submitted to the App Store by other developers, with only minor differences… we encourage you to review the app concept and submit a unique app with distinct content and functionality." Stated contributing factors: same source code or assets as other apps; multiple similar apps from a repackaged template; a template with problematic code bought from a third party; "submitting several similar apps across multiple accounts." A variant targets apps resembling those "previously submitted by a terminated Apple Developer Program account."

Sources: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825522 (search-indexed; direct fetch blocked) · https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/775805 (search-indexed; direct fetch blocked) · https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/757046 (search-indexed)

### 6.3 What makes a reviewer call an app non-unique

1. **Concept-level resemblance, even with original code.** A forum reply from a peer: "Spam does not necessarily mean that your code is not original code, but could also mean the app concept or design [is] too close to existing ones." (thread/757046)
2. **Binary + metadata + concept are all compared; cosmetics don't register.** "App Review compares the binary, metadata, and concept, and minor differences in color, text, or naming do not register as uniqueness." (PTKD)
3. **Category saturation, independent of copying.** "This is about market saturation rather than copying a specific app, so even an original take on a crowded idea can be refused if it does not clearly stand out." (PTKD, on 4.3(b))
4. **Template/AI-shaped structure.** "When hundreds of developers use the same AI tool… the results look similar enough that Apple's review team treats them as clutter." (ReleaseMyApp)
5. **Binary fingerprints and shared dependency provenance.** A developer who took a 4.3(a) rejection through four appeals reports Apple's tooling "looks at the compiled binary, dependency graph, asset hashes, and overall app concept"; shared forks of another developer's libraries embedded in dependency metadata "trip the scan." (Medium)
6. **History matters.** Prior rejected submissions can flag an account for extra scrutiny (ReleaseMyApp); apps similar to a terminated account's apps get a specific 4.3(a) variant (forum/757046).

### 6.4 Standard patterns that clear it

1. **Consolidate duplicates into one app with configuration/IAP** (Apple's stated preference; echoed by practitioner guides). (guidelines fetch; PTKD)
2. **Substantive, visible differentiation, not decoration.** "Uniqueness has to come from functionality, a distinct workflow, or content that a reviewer can see sets the app apart… A reskin… is still a clone." (PTKD; ReleaseMyApp: "different enough that it's obvious within the first 30 seconds")
3. **A complete appeal package**: feature comparison table against the apps Apple might think you duplicate; annotated screenshots of unique UI/workflows; technical implementation details; user validation; target-audience differentiation. (ReleaseMyApp)
4. **Appeal mechanics:** one appeal per rejection with specific compliance arguments (Apple staff answer, forum/825522); escalate to the App Review Board when Resolution Center loops stall — the successful case after 4 rejections landed by adding "this app serves a genuine audience with no alternative," i.e., arguing why the app must exist. (Medium; forum/825522)
5. **Don't resubmit cosmetically or hastily**: no icon renames, no same-binary resubmits, no bolting on unrelated features; reviewers can see submission timelines. (ReleaseMyApp)
6. **Keep your own binary/dependency provenance** (own forks, own assets, English-language permissions letters where relevant). (Medium; forum/825522 factors)

### 6.5 Applying this to Cab Hustle (game-specific reading)

- The endless traffic genre is exactly the "crowded idea" case: near-name-alike store listings exist in bulk (§1.10), and the top title's own name is reused by third parties. Cab Hustle's concept must therefore present at first glance as "arcade taxi game with bank-or-push and a daily ghost shift," not as "another endless traffic racer." The concept is comparable to competitors while the mechanics (bank-or-push, three-strike, date-seeded ghost, taxi economy) are not — that difference has to be in the screenshots, the subtitle and the review notes, because reviewers compare concept/metadata, not code intent. (guidelines fetch; PTKD; forum/825522)
- Metadata must not resemble the leaders' language ("endless arcade racing," highway/traffic emphasis). Apple's 4.1(a) and the 2025 4.1(c) addition (no other developer's icon, brand or product name) apply directly to avoiding the Traffic Racer / Crazy Taxi naming gravity. (guidelines fetch; https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/13/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines-to-crack-down-on-copycat-apps/ — direct)
- Review notes should spell out, with annotated screenshots: the bank-or-push decision, the three-strike lives, the Daily Shift seed + ghost, the near-miss economy, the handling-different garage — the things no comparator has. (ReleaseMyApp appeal pattern applied)
- Keep the app's binary/deps clean: Flutter/Flame are ubiquitous engines, not another developer's app, but no assets, sounds or project code should trace to another shipped game. (Medium fingerprint account; forum/825522 factor list)

## 7. Gaps that matter most, ranked for the product run

1. **A career ladder with named objectives** (Traffic Rider's 90+ missions is the bar). Cab Hustle's tutorial ladder + records don't create "next mission" pull. No network needed.
2. **An upgrade economy visible in the HUD.** Moto Rider GO's speed/braking/extra-lives stats and Traffic Racer's cash → car loop are the pattern. Cab Hustle's garage exists; make cash buys and stat deltas explicit. No network needed.
3. **Daily Shift payout.** The daily mission/daily quest/passive income patterns exist to make day 2 pay specifically; Cab Hustle's Daily Shift is a stronger idea than most — its rewards must be specific (streak record, exclusive title, ghost beats). No network needed.
4. **A failure decision.** Drift Boss sells the second chance at the exact moment of failure; Cab Hustle's third strike should be a decision moment (e.g., bank a smaller score or push one more street). No network needed.
5. **Mode breadth.** One extra mode (one-life time attack or fare rush) buys weeks of goals for little content. No network needed.
6. **Identity.** Dashy Crashy proves tone (announcer, low-poly constraint) carries a store page; a cab game has claim on the tone the genre's own origin story references (harrowing taxi rides). No network needed.
7. **Uniqueness framing is a shippable feature.** The no-ads/no-IAP, fully on-device, bank-or-push taxi is structurally different from every studied game; that belongs in the subtitle, screenshots and review notes as much as in the product. (This is the 4.3(a) play per §6.5.)
